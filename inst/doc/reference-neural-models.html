Reference models and native R neural survival engines

Informative references

Kaplan-Meier ignores covariates and provides a probability baseline. Its constant risk scores give concordance 0.5 when comparable pairs exist. Cox with natural splines checks whether smooth nonlinear effects explain an apparent advantage of complex learners.

d <- survival::veteran
f <- Surv(time, status) ~ age + karno
km <- surv_fit(f, d, "km")
smooth <- surv_fit(f, d, "cox_spline", params = list(df = 3))
surv_compare(list(km = km, smooth = smooth), d)
#>    model  metric time  estimate lower upper   n events status message
#> 1     km harrell   NA 0.5000000    NA    NA 137    128     ok        
#> 2 smooth harrell   NA 0.7013289    NA    NA 137    128     ok

These are resubstitution examples, not held-out performance claims. Training spline bases are reused at prediction. Covariate splines do not remove PH.

flexsurv provides Royston-Parmar hazard, odds or normal-scale models. coxboost is componentwise likelihood boosting, distinct from gbm/XGBoost trees. ranger is an alternative forest engine; aorsf provides oblique forest splits.

install.packages(c("flexsurv", "CoxBoost", "ranger", "aorsf"))
surv_fit(f, d, "flexsurv", list(knots = 1, scale = "hazard"))
surv_fit(f, d, "coxboost", list(steps = 100, penalty = 100))
surv_fit(f, d, "ranger", list(trees = 200))
surv_fit(f, d, "aorsf", list(trees = 200))

Neural Cox

For \(\lambda(t\mid x)=\lambda_0(t)\exp\{f_\theta(x)\}\), a neural network replaces the linear predictor. With untied events, the loss is

\[ -\sum_{i:\delta_i=1}\left[f_\theta(x_i)- \log\left\{\sum_{j:Y_j\geq Y_i}\exp(f_\theta(x_j))\right\}\right] +\text{regularization}. \]

This remains PH. Nonlinear covariate effects are not time-varying hazard ratios. A Breslow baseline estimated on training converts scores to probabilities.

The native adapter uses R matrix operations and analytic backpropagation, with no Torch, Python, or external neural runtime. A single tanh hidden layer and linear log-risk output are trained by full-batch Adam. The penalty is \(\texttt{decay}\|\theta\|^2/2\), including biases. Training uses double precision. Fixed epochs do not establish convergence; the final objective and maximum absolute gradient are exposed in diagnostics.

At each distinct event time, let \(D_g\) be the deaths, \(d_g=|D_g|\), \(R_g\) the entire risk set (including censorings at that time), \(S_g=\sum_{j\in R_g}\exp(f_\theta(x_j))\) and \(E_g=\sum_{j\in D_g}\exp(f_\theta(x_j))\). Efron’s contribution to the log partial likelihood is

\[ \sum_{i\in D_g} f_\theta(x_i)- \sum_{l=0}^{d_g-1}\log\{S_g-(l/d_g)E_g\}. \]

Breslow uses \(d_g\log S_g\) instead of the second sum. The loss averages over the observed deaths. The baseline increment is the sum of the corresponding inverse denominators, so prediction uses the same ties convention as training. No times are jittered. Stable log-domain risk sums and baseline storage avoid overflow. These approximations are not the exact discrete partial likelihood.

fit <- surv_fit(f, training, "neural_cox",
  params = list(hidden = 16, epochs = 100, learning_rate = 0.001,
    decay = 0.0001, ties = "efron"))
surv_predict(fit, new_equipment, "survival", times = c(30, 60, 90))

Discrete-time neural survival

Let \(h_k(x)=P(T\in I_k\mid T\geq\inf I_k,x)\). PLANN models interval failure probabilities with covariates and interval information. Its likelihood uses failures and known survival through completed intervals; censored rows are not labeled as failures. Endpoint survival is

\[ S(t_k\mid x)=\prod_{\ell\leq k}\{1-h_\ell(x)\}. \]

Time in a nonlinear network can permit non-PH effects. Interval construction, within-interval censoring and resolution remain substantive modeling choices. The adapter constructs person-period rows and uses nnet’s Bernoulli entropy loss. Events contribute their failure interval; censoring contributes only fully completed intervals. An incomplete censoring interval is never labeled as survived. This coarsening discards within-interval timing information and can introduce discretization bias. A canonical fixed-size grid and scaling are learned on training; endpoint probabilities are returned as a step function. Risk is negative restricted mean on that grid, not an extrapolated full mean.

install.packages("nnet")
fit <- surv_fit(f, d, "neural_discrete",
  params = list(hidden = 8, intervals = 10, decay = 0.01, max_iter = 500))
surv_predict(fit, d[1:2, ], "survival", times = c(30, 60, 90))
surv_diagnostics(fit)

Heavy engines are not needed for core models. Grids must be explicit and the final test must not choose training duration. Saved models are tested in fresh R sessions; live external pointers do not provide general serialization.
